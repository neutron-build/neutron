## Is a `--keep` relaunch safe after an interrupted contained run?
**Yes for T3's scope stop, and fail-closed for a SIGKILLed runner. Neither is a defence against the routes -F2 owns.**

- **The fact that decides this:** `unshare --fork` blocks SIGTERM (`SigBlk 0x4002`) and an init ignores a signal it has no handler for. So a scope's SIGTERM does not end a container. The runner's `finally` does, through the pidfd.
- **Scope stop, measured with real `systemctl --user stop`.** The pidfd was found in 26 of 26 end paths. The last member stamp was 0.4 to 1.0 ms before the end path returned (bare harness, 20 runs, 5 of them with unshare signalled first). On real `main()`, the stamp preceded the HEAD read by 0.5 to 23 ms (`stamps1.txt`). A forge committing from its TERM trap, at delays from 0 to 150 ms, in opencode's container and in lines with small and 4 GB inits: 18 runs, plus one earlier, and none landed after the end record (`scan1.txt`). When one landed in time, `worker_commits` counted it as the worker's. The live card also went through this for real during this gate: X-5c-F4-F1-F1's ledger closed at 21:35:50 and at 23:15:23, the second within a second of `Stopping t3code-claude-a075eb97.scope`.
- **Runner SIGKILLed outside a scope stop, measured (`sigkill.txt`).** The container lives on. A `--keep` relaunch passed beside it, and the leftover's forge landed after that relaunch's end record. **The SIGKILLed run's range never closes, so the forge counted as a worker's commit**, and the third run failed with `Scope violations: verdicts/PRB.md (protected)`. Fail-closed, but two runs share the worktree and a leftover keeps writing to the card's run logs. **Orchestrator rule:** when a ledger's last run has no end record, look for an `unshare` with that worktree as cwd and end it before relaunching. A scope stop that runs into its SIGKILL leaves the same open range, with nothing alive.
- **F3 is the one real gap, by reading:** a SIGTERM inside the spawn leaves a container nothing knows, past the end record. About a millisecond per spawn, against runs of hours.

## Findings
**F1. `end_gate`'s init wait is right and unpinned.** FG-END-NO-WAIT passes all 827 rows. On `end_gate` alone with a 1 GB init (`fgwait/results.txt`): with the wait, the member's last stamp came 16 to 43 ms before return; without it, 77 to 112 ms after. So the code is right and the row's gate is too small to feel the difference. Non-blocking.

**F2. The probe's `timeout 30` bounds nothing.** `timeout 1 unshare ... sleep 30` returned after 30 s, for the reason above. A hung probe hangs fresh-gate, which prints no result, so this fails closed. `containment_problem` uses `subprocess.run(timeout=30)`, whose kill is SIGKILL, and is fine.

**F3. The spawn window, by reading, not run.** A SIGTERM during `Popen`'s exec wait, or between its return and `child = ...`, raises with `child` still the earlier one. The new container keeps running and its commits land in no range. Present for opencode since X-6-F1-F1-F2-F1; this card adds one spawn per line and the same window. Inside a scope stop the scope's SIGKILL ends it after up to 90 s, not at once.

**F4. PID 1, narrower than reported.** dash forks a plain command, so a fatal self-signal from one still works: `os.kill(getpid(), SIGKILL)` exits 137 in and out of the container (also SIGTERM, abort and a segfault, measured). Only the top shell's own `$$` and an `exec`'d program are affected: `exec python3 -c '...SIGKILL...; print("survived")'` printed `survived` and exited 0, where plain it exits 137. A line failing only that way would pass. 0 of 127 cards have one, and no crate or script self-signals (`git grep`). Non-blocking: -F1 states it.

**By reading, not run:** Ctrl-C at a terminal no longer stops `fresh-gate.sh` promptly: `unshare` blocks SIGINT and the init ignores it, so a step's children die and `gate.sh` moves to its next step, ending in `GATE EXIT=1`. Gates run non-interactively, so it is noted only.
