## Correctness review: dropping `P` before a delete's free

The card's semantic change is sound. Each delete (settler (a)'s abort cleanup,
`T`'s `ROLLBACK TO` delete, a replayed `Delete{revert}`) keeps the leaf's exclusive
latch and the shared epoch gate from the entry's removal to the last chain LPID's
free. Releasing `P` earlier changes none of the following:

- **Readers** reach the entry only through the leaf's latch, so they never see the
  entry gone while its chain is still mapped, or the reverse. The other case is a
  reader that reads a chain after releasing the leaf latch (§6.7, "Reading without the
  leaf latch"). No other transaction reads an `Open` or `Aborted` owner's tag-8 chain
  (C-T0 v8 §7.1: "readers ignore those entries because the owner is `Aborted`"), so an
  abort's free removes nothing another reader holds. `T`'s own reads during
  `ROLLBACK TO` are unchanged by this card: the free was already in the deleting
  operation.
- **Concurrent writers**, in three cases:
  - A pack or merge of this leaf latches `P`, then this leaf, so it waits on the
    leaf's latch.
  - A sibling split may now install a separator in `P` during the free. That does not
    change this leaf's range.
  - A racing settler (c) on the same dead entry is serialised by the leaf's latch, and
    both deletes are conditional on identity (C-T0 v8 §2.4), so the second does
    nothing.
- **Cuts**: the gate is held shared throughout, so an image has the entry and its
  chain or neither (I-LINK-ATOMIC, unchanged). The abort cleanup writes no WAL
  (C-T0 v8 §7.1), and the card adds none.
- **Restart**: the delete reads cold chain pages and MapLeafs in place under the leaf
  latch only, which §5.3 allows (that rule covers inner latches), so it never needs a
  restart after its first mutation.

**Anomaly ruling: "Such a delete is not among §6.5's pack callers".** As a
citation, the sentence is not true. §6.5's second bullet says "**A delete that
descends from the root under latch coupling** ... may pack". All three deletes
descend from the root under latch coupling, and only the parenthesised example
("a directory removal") leaves them out. The sentence treats an example as the
complete list.

The behaviour it licenses is still correct, for three reasons:
- §6.5 permits packing ("may pack") and does not require it.
- Under §6.2 these deletes could never pack in the first place. §3.2 makes a page
  hold at least 4 maximum-size cells, so `C_max ≤ U/4`. An unsafe leaf (the only kind
  on which coupling keeps `P`) has `used > U − C_max ≥ 3U/4`. After deleting one cell,
  `used > U/2`, which is above the `U/4` pack threshold. On a leaf where `used < U/4`,
  coupling had already released `P`.
- Once the delete has released `P` it is "a delete made while holding only a leaf"
  (§6.5's third bullet), which never packs.

So this is a wording defect, not a spec conflict or an invariant break. No ESCALATE.
It belongs in this card's rework (R2), because the sentence is this card's own text,
inside its Limits 1, and the fix needs no edit of §6.5. -F3's planned parenthesis,
"(no caller in 2.0: ...)", is consistent with the reworded sentence and needs no
change. -F3's gate should still read the two together.

## Findings (REWORK)

**R1. The build variant cannot catch CHAIN-BUILD-UNDER-P as written. This defeats
the card's Goal 2.**

The setup says: "the MapLeafs of the next free LPIDs the new chain takes are
evicted". Here is why that does not work:
- Allocation takes the lowest free LPID (§4.2).
- The split allocates first. It pins the MapLeaf of its own LPID before its first
  mutation (§5.6, §6.4).
- Its new page is then dirty, so that MapLeaf stays resident (§4.1).
- The chain's LPIDs are the next ones. They share that MapLeaf unless the test
  places a MapLeaf boundary between them, and a MapLeaf holds 510 entries at 8 KiB,
  so sharing is the normal case.
- So when the build runs, its MapLeaf is resident. The mutant reads nothing evicted
  under `P`, the hook stays silent, and the mutant survives. That is the same kind of
  gap judgment call 1 of `verdicts/S-1-F7-F2.md` found.

There is a second way to hide the build. The variant inherits the base setup, an
evicted chain-bearing old value. If the old chain is freed first, the build reuses
its LPIDs, whose MapLeafs the free has just loaded. If the mutant instead keeps `P`
for both, the hook fires on the free's read, so the catch is credited to the wrong
half.

Fix, inside Limit 4: the build variant must take the new chain's LPIDs from a
MapLeaf other than those of the split's allocations, for example by making the
split's LPID the last free entry of its MapLeaf. The old value must be inline, or its
chain resident, so the only cold read in the variant is the build's own MapLeaf.

**R2. Reword "Such a delete is not among §6.5's pack callers, so it needs no
ancestor ...", inside Limit 1.**

Suggested text: "Such a delete does not pack: it releases `P` and every other
ancestor before it deletes, so it is a delete made while holding only a leaf (§6.5)
and leaves an under-full leaf to the sweep." Update grep 4 to match. The reasoning
is in the ruling above.

**R3. Pin the rewritten text, in the card's Acceptance.** Add greps that fail on
U1, U4 and U5. U1 is the "An insert releases `P` and every other ancestor once any
split has installed its separators, and only then replaces the entry and frees the
old chain." sentence. U4 is the delete variants' eviction clause. U5 is the build
variant's "the rewrite splits" and the new R1 clause. Pins for U2, U3 and U7 are
cheap and also recommended.

R1–R3 change the card's verbatim Work text and its Acceptance, so the card's author
(P) has to amend the card before a worker reruns it. The worker did what the card
said. R1 is a defect in the card's prescribed text, not the implementation.
