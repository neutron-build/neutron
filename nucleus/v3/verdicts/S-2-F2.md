It justifies this with "a writer appends to a segment only after its preallocation succeeded". But that premise only says the call *returned*. So here is the failure:
1. A segment is recycled by rename (§12.1), or newly created and `set_len`'d.
2. Records in it are appended, `sync_data`'d and acked.
3. A power cut then loses the unsynced rename, the create, or the length.
4. Recovery finds the segment missing or short, stops cleanly, and drops acked commits without a word. That breaks I-REDO-COMPLETE.

In this scenario the mutant SHORT-SEG-ERROR (58030) is the safer behaviour. The fix is one clause in the same sentence (R6). The re-gate can be limited to §12.5's sentence and its changelog line.

## R1–R5 and the nit

- **R1: met.** §14.1's new paragraph covers four things:
  - **The cause.** A 53100-stopped store is a crashed store on disk, because poisoning is in memory only (§9.2).
  - **Every write point.** WAL append, segment creation and preallocation, file extension, checkpoint page write, meta write. Each one is mapped to past the valid end, an unreferenced slot or extension, or the losing meta slot. I checked each mapping against §2.2, §4.5, §12.5 and §14 step 2, and they are sound.
  - **The result.** The open succeeds at zero free bytes, writes nothing, and keeps the data exportable, subject to the evictable pool minimum.
  - **The test.** The I-RO-OPEN row names `t_ro_open_disk_full` (A-7d) in full shape:
    - ENOSPC at each of the six write points in turn;
    - a SimEnv with zero free bytes that refuses every write, including a lock file's creation;
    - the open succeeds, reads equal a read-write open of a copy on a roomy Env, every published tree scans fully, and the files are byte-identical after close;
    - the 53200 case writes nothing, then a larger-pool read-only open succeeds at zero free bytes.
- **R2: met.** "The remedy for 53200" now includes a read-only open with a larger pool budget (`F` per open from N16, §5.1). A new bullet says that on a volume with no free space this is the only remedy, because the read-write open's boot checkpoint fails with 53100 before its meta is durable and leaves the state unchanged. Nit, not blocking: "fails there" should read "can fail there". With enough internal free slots, a boot checkpoint may need no filesystem growth. So the read-only open is the only *guaranteed* remedy.
- **R3: met.** "The lock creates and writes no file", in either mode. It is an advisory lock on the page file itself, or OPFS's `read-only` access handle against a writer's exclusive `readwrite`. Mutant RO-LOCK-FILE. Nit for A-1: an `O_RDONLY` fd cannot take a POSIX `fcntl` write lock. The read-only open needs a shared (read) lock mode, and PLAN-v3 §3.2's `Env` only has `lock_exclusive` (see the proposed follow-up).
- **R4: text present, premise defective (R6).** The sentence is there, the mutant SHORT-SEG-ERROR is named in the I-RO-OPEN row and its mutant column, and the writer rule is stated. But the writer rule is stated as "succeeded", not "durable".

## Rulings on the two extensions

1. **Covering MISSING segments as well as short ones (R1/R4): accepted in scope, conditional on R6.** It is in the same sentence and the amended Limits. A segment whose creation never reached the directory is the same case as a short one, and R1's segment-creation stop point needs it. But a missing segment is told apart from a *lost* acked one only by the durability of its create or rename. So the extension is safe only once R6 lands. Optional hardening (A-6b may choose): if the reader stops at a missing or short segment while a later segment's first record is valid with `lsn` equal to its first LSN, return 58030.
2. **The two new mutant names: accepted.** RO-LOCK-FILE and SHORT-SEG-ERROR each name one falsifiable deviation, and each is caught by `t_ro_open_disk_full` as written. They sit in the I-RO-OPEN mutant column, which is right, since both break the read-only open first.

## Rework item

- **R6 (must, §12.5, the same sentence, and its changelog line).** Replace the premise "a writer appends to a segment only after its preallocation succeeded" with a durability rule. A writer acks no record in a segment until the segment's creation or recycling rename and its preallocated length are **durable**: the segment `sync_all`'d, and its `wal/` directory entry synced through `Env`, or the platform's equivalent. Only then is "nothing acked lies past that point" true. Without R6, a power cut that loses an unsynced create or rename turns acked commits into a clean stop (SEG-ACK-BEFORE-DURABLE, uncaught today).
