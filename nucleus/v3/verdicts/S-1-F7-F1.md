No pool, pin or WAL bound uses it: §5.4, `R_MAP` and the DropTree pins count tree pages and MapLeaves, not chain pages, and I-OP-BOUND uses `ROW_LIMIT` itself.
- **Work 3, the shape.** A step that marks a chain-bearing entry ends before its first mutation, which is legal (§5.6). Each chain then goes in its own gated operation, holding only its leaf. A delete never grows a leaf, so coupling may drop the parent, and chain pages and MapLeaves are read in place with no inner latch held, as §5.6 allows. This mirrors S-1-F7's §6.8 shape.
- **Read-equivalence between the separate deletes.** Each entry deleted alone is an older version shadowed by a kept `≤ w` version or by a tombstone that stays until later, or a dead provisional entry that readers ignore from its bytes. A read at `S ≥ W` meets the shadowing entry first. No registered reader holds such a chain without its latch (§6.7 reads only the newest `≤ S`). The §8.2 probes look only at group heads, which are never deleted here. I-GC-ORDER holds: an ending group's tombstone goes in the re-run's mutation, after its chain-bearing older versions, and a pending tombstone goes after the step that passes its group's end.
- **Crash cuts.** Each separate delete is a GC mutation, so §10.5 raises `gc_w_used` for it. Its chain is freed in its own operation (I-LINK-ATOMIC). An image cut between two of them holds a read-equivalent state that leaks nothing. The token is in memory, so a crash only re-sweeps, which is under-pruning.
- **Termination and livelock.** The re-runs end, but for a reason the text only half gives (R1). Each re-descent's `L ∪ R` is a prefix of the earlier window plus new inserts, because only this sweep packs a user tree and splits only narrow the window. The inserts are versions `> w` or current-boot provisional entries, and neither is ever marked. Older versions `≤ w` are never created (§10.1). Dead provisional entries are never created either, but the reason is different: dead means a previous boot, and no current-boot entry ever becomes dead. So the marked chain-bearing set shrinks every round, and concurrent writers cannot livelock the step. The text cites only §10.1, which excludes provisional entries.

## Findings (REWORK)

- **R1. The per-entry delete must be conditional on the entry's identity, and the shape must be pinned for that.** §10.4 says each chain-bearing entry is deleted "by entry key" and does nothing only "if the entry is already gone". A provisional entry's key is `entry_key(k, u64::MAX)`, and C-T0 v8 §2.4 lets a writer on `k` settle a dead entry and then write its own. Before this card, the sweep marked and deleted a dead tag-8 entry under one latch hold. Now the latches are released between the step's end and each delete, so a writer can do exactly that in the gap. A literal "by entry key" delete would then delete an `Open` owner's live entry and free its chain. C-T0 v8 §2.4 does make every settler (c) delete conditional, the sweep included, but §10.4's own sentence reads otherwise, and no test or mutant pins it on this path (G2 UNCAUGHT). Fix, within Limits 2 (§10.4's chain sentences, the I-GC rows):
  - (a) Say that each separate delete is conditional on identity (C-T0 v8 §2.4). A version is identified by its entry key (I-ENTRY-UNIQUE). A tag-8 entry must still be dead, checked from its bytes under the leaf's exclusive latch. A current-boot entry at that key is left alone.
  - (b) Give "Why the re-runs end" its second reason: dead entries come only from previous boots, and current-boot entries never become dead.
  - (c) Add two things to `t_sweep_step_shape`'s fixture: a writer that, between the step's end and the delete, settles a dead tag-8 entry and writes its own; and an elided-tombstone group whose older versions hold chains, read between operations. Name CHAIN-DELETE-BY-KEY in the row. G3 then fails it through I-GC-ORDER.

## Ruling on the implementer's escalation (§10.3)

**It is real.** §6.4 says a leaf that cannot take an insert runs the §10.3 prune first. The prune deletes, in one page mutation, every version that a newer `≤ w` version on the same leaf shadows, and it elides tombstones together with their groups' older versions. §6.7 frees each deleted entry's chain in the same gated operation. §5.6 runs the prune under the insert's hold.

The leaf is full, so it is not *safe* (§6.2), and coupling still holds `P`, and possibly more ancestors, exclusively. If the prune is the insert's first mutation, every chain page it frees afterwards is read under `P`'s exclusive latch. §5.3 forbids that, and §5.6 forbids reading in place once an inner latch is held. The operation cannot restart after that mutation, either.

**Repro (on paper).** Fill a toast-region leaf with superseded chunk versions and their tombstones `≤ w`. At 8 KiB a leaf holds over 100 such cells, and each chunk version holds a 32-page chain. Evict the chains, then insert into that leaf. One gated operation now does thousands of cold reads under `P`, and the flip waits for all of them. A hot key whose value exceeds `V_INLINE` and is updated often does the same with up to about 130 pages per version.

The same holds for a prune at eviction, which may run nested inside another operation's hold (§5.2). The defect predates this card: §10.3 is outside its Limits, and this card neither causes nor worsens it.

**This card may merge with it open** once R1 and R2 are fixed. Its §10.4 shape is a strict improvement, and R2 makes C-T6 and §6.7 true while the prune is still unfixed.

## Follow-up

- **S-1-F7-F1-F1: the page-local prune frees no chain under an inner latch (S-STORE §10.3, §6.4, §5.2; C-T6 §4.3).** Class: spec, Gate: P.
  - **The defect.** §10.3's prune, which must run before a split (§6.4) and may run at eviction, deletes chain-bearing versions and dead tag-8 entries. It frees their chains in the same gated operation (§6.7). Under a splitting insert, it runs with `P` (and possibly more ancestors) exclusively latched (§6.2), so it reads chain pages under an inner latch (against §5.3 and §5.6) and frees up to a leaf's worth of chains in one operation. That is the defect S-1-F7 closed for DropTree and this card closed for the sweep.
  - **The design direction.** The prune frees no chain. It deletes and elides only what holds no chain reference: tags 0–3 and dead tags 6–7. A newest-`≤ w` tombstone is elided only when none of its group's older versions on the leaf still holds a chain, so I-GC-ORDER holds. Chain-bearing garbage is left to the sweep's "Chains first". A splitting insert may instead do what §10.4 does, before its first mutation: release everything, delete each chain-bearing prunable entry in its own leaf-only gated operation (conditional on identity), and restart. The card picks one and gives its liveness argument. A prune at eviction only ever skips chain-bearing entries.
  - **What it adds.** The prune-under-split operation joins A-5's lists in S-STORE §6.7 and C-T6 §4.3 if the restart variant is chosen. Otherwise R2's interim clause is removed. It also names a mutant, PRUNE-FREES-CHAINS (the prune frees chains under the splitting insert's hold), with a test in I-GC-LOCAL's row: a full toast-region leaf of superseded chunks with cold chains, and an insert that splits it, with a hook counting chain frees per operation and checking the latches held at each chain page read.
  - **What must not change.** The rule that a split happens only after a prune (§6.4); I-GC-LOCAL and PRUNE-FIRST-ENTRY; I-GC-READEQ, I-GC-ORDER, I-LINK-ATOMIC; §6.7's build-after-room rule; §5.3 and §5.6; S-1-F7's §6.8 shape and this card's §10.4 shape; any byte layout or persisted state; `ROW_LIMIT`, unless A-5 measures a reason.
- **Observation, no card.** A chain delete holding only its leaf blocks that leaf's readers for the chain's length. A-5 may report the leaf-latch hold beside the flip hold.
