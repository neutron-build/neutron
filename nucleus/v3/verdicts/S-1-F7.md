## Findings

**F1. §6.8 step 3 and §5.6 contradict each other (flagged item 1).** §5.6 still says, unqualified, "An operation that restarts releases the gate and re-acquires it." §6.8 step 3 says that release "applies only before an operation's first mutation". §5.6 has no such words. An implementer of §5.6's gate discipline follows §5.6 and reopens the exact straddle this card closes, and §5.3's "restarts from the root" points the same way. The need is not local to DropTree either: §6.7's new "Building" rule has it too (a chain mapped before the insert's descent, then a non-resident leaf), and §6.7 says nothing, so A-5 would follow §5.6 and get a leak-direction cut.

**F2. The invariant's last clause is false by the card's own text (flagged item 2).** §5.6: "§4.2's immediate reuse never hands out an LPID something still names." §6.8 step 3: "a concurrent allocation may take the freed LPID (§4.2) while the entry still names it." The justification, "the tree's one walk, the only reader of a `dropping` entry's root", appears nowhere else in S-STORE and is not established:
- **One walk.** Nothing states it. C-T6 §7.4's "A DropTree of an absent or `dropping` tree only resumes the walk" reads as a second walker if reached online, and C-T6 §5.3's ORPHAN-LIST-FIRST names "a second time beside the walk" as a real hazard. By my reading of the callers the claim does hold today: the drop job's ops and row delete are one record, the orphan job skips `dropping`, and step 6 runs once. But it needs stating.
- **Only reader, step 7.** §14 step 7's amcheck follows step 6's background walks with nothing ordering them. It can read the entry inside the window and report an unmapped root, which draft 7 itself defines as XX001. amcheck is "on a quiescent file" (§15), and the step order does not make it one.
- **Only reader, stale `live` reads.** C-T6 §7.5 says "a running sweep stops at its next step" and "a cleanup delete in a `dropping` or absent tree does nothing". Both are check-then-act, and both hold the gate shared, as the walk does, so the gate excludes nothing here. S-STORE does not say how a thread gets a root LPID: §6.2 says "a re-descent from the root", and §6.3 says "readers need no root re-check". A sweep that reads `live`, is preempted while drop cleanup commits and its walk finishes on a small tree, and resumes will latch the reused LPID as its root and apply §10.2 with carried key state to another tree's page. This race predates the card (draft 6 has it too), but it falsifies the clause the card now states universally. The window is the narrower case; this one is why the fix needs a rule and not only a reorder.

What does hold: the walk has indeed finished with the root; concurrent splits cannot hurt the walk; and recovery resume is safe, because the window never reaches an image (no flip inside it) and a resumed walk starts from a closed image.

**The evidence does not reach this.** The prototype runs on a baton scheduler with a per-tree mutex (`tree_lock`, `prims.rs:328`; `tree_lock(cx, DIR_TREE)` in `dir_remove_entry_held`), not latch coupling. Its `descend` under the merge toggle has no restart, and `tree.rs` has no overflow chains. So the 28-round result says nothing about F1, F2 or F3. The census itself I re-derived with `evidence/attr.py`: byte-identical to `census-51.txt`, 27 + 1 = 28.

**F3. §6.8 step 2's new wording breaks the "bounded" and "one directory descent" claims.** "Each step unlinks a child from its parent and frees it, with its entries' overflow chains, in one gated operation" makes a whole leaf's chains one gated operation. Step 3 repeats it for a leaf root. Draft 6 did not require this, and I-LINK-ATOMIC does not either: an entry and its chain are already one operation by §6.7.
- **Size (my estimate, assuming about 50 bytes per chain-reference cell at 8 KiB).** A toast-region leaf holds about 160 entries of 32 pages each (`toast_chunk` = 256 KiB), about 5,000 pages per step, on an ordinary drop of a toasted table. The `ROW_LIMIT` worst case is about 160 × 128, about 20,000 pages. A-5 measures one chain, 128 pages.
- **Cost.** Each page is read for its next pointer, and a dropped table is typically cold. At an assumed 100 µs per read that is about 0.5 s to 2 s of flip hold per step, with every new gated operation queued behind the pending flip.
- **§5.3.** The step holds the parent exclusively, an inner page, across those reads, which §5.3 forbids. Releasing it instead means another restart after a mutation, which is F1 again.
- **Card claim.** The Goal's "costing the flip at most one directory descent" is therefore false for this text.

**F4. The tests do not pin the invariant as written.** §15's preamble requires a test and a named mutant per invariant. The invariant names four reference kinds. Mutants cover a child pointer (one direction) and a directory root. An entry's chain reference and an Overflow page's next pointer have none, and §6.7 "Building" and §12.8 "Pages" have no test at all. `t_drop_every_cut`'s fixture has no chains and no non-resident directory page, and its cuts are "between a DropTree's gated steps", so it cannot catch the most likely implementation error: step 3 releasing the gate on a restart.

**F5. §5.6's new claim does not say try-latch.** "claims the oldest copy's frame under the frame's latch" can be read as a blocking acquire. Blocking makes an ABBA in a real engine: writer W holds MapLeaf M's latch in `write_guard` (map pages use it, §4.1), and blocks on copy C's latch; exhauster B holds C's latch and needs M to record the copy in the image map (§5.2). The prototype's global `st.map.img` cannot show this. §5.2 already says "never blocks on a victim", so one clause closes it. It also needs the case where every copy is claimed: `R_EPOCH` must exceed what threads can claim at once, or a writer waits holding a page latch.

**F6. The Acceptance greps do not pin the claims (spec mutation, on a scratch copy).** All 8 terms are absent at base. But all 8 still pass with each of these applied: M1, the §5.6 definition deleted; M2, §6.8 step 3 restored to "Remove the directory entry", the defect itself; M3, the §15 row deleted; M4, §14 step 4 changed to say pass 2 walks. ## Rulings on the flagged items

1. **No deadlock, but a different shape.** Holding the gate there cannot deadlock. A gate holder waits only on latches, pins and I/O. A latch holder either holds the gate (never twice, §5.6, so it finishes) or holds no gate and never blocks on it (§5.2). A pin wait is finite by §5.4, because threads parked on the gate hold at most their `height + 2` quota. Eviction under an outer hold takes victims with try_latch. The free-set lock is held across no wait. The flip waits only for the holder. The hold is one descent's reads, at most height + 1. Even so, the keep-gate exception should go. **Required shape:** pin the root and the directory path before the first mutation, where a restart may release the gate harmlessly. Then, in one hold, remove the entry first and free the root (and any emptied pages) after it. §5.6 then states the general rule: an operation restarts only before its first mutation. This also removes the window (item 2), keeps every read before the mutation phase, and costs nothing: once the entry is gone the pages are unreachable.
2. **The claim is not safe as stated; see F2.** The reorder makes the invariant's last clause true in memory for the window and retires the "one walk" argument. Two sentences are still required for the rest: at most one walk per tree (a DropTree of a tree with a running walk does nothing), and a thread takes a root LPID only under the latch of the directory leaf holding its entry, latches the root before releasing that leaf, and keeps no root LPID across gated operations.
3. **Confirmed.** §6.1's `n+1` children make zero children the only illegal inner shape, a step frees any parent it empties, and a one-child inner page satisfies §6.1. All remaining leaves stay at one depth. amcheck's list (§15) excludes I-SPACE, so no exemption is needed. An empty non-root leaf, which F3's fix can produce, is not forbidden by §6.1.
4. **Both placements accepted.** EXHAUST-DOUBLE-EVICT loses image content, so I-CKPT-AT-T is the right row. REPLAY-DROP-WALK in I-RO-OPEN, with the pages-stay-mapped assertion, is right.
5. **Yes, follow-up:** S-1-F7-F1. C-T6 §4.3 and its N4 row still give A-5 only the free and quote the old §6.7. That card also takes the wider point that A-5 must measure per gated operation, and §10.4's sweep step, which frees two leaves' chains under `P`'s latch and is outside this card's limits.
