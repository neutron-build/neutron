## Findings
**F-a. The deadlock search's due time restarts at every retry, so a cycle can outlive `deadlock_timeout` without bound** (S-2a-1-F4; I-LIVE (b), (d)).
- **The text.** "Once a wait has lasted `deadlock_timeout` … otherwise it does not search again in this wait". A wait is one call of `wait_for`. Every wake from a holder's release returns through step 3, and W "re-runs the acquire", which makes a new wait. I-LIVE (d) repeats this: "searches once its wait has lasted `deadlock_timeout`".
- **The scenario, with no race.**
  - `W2` waits on `W1`. Its search falls due first, finds no cycle, and is never repeated: `W1` releases nothing, so nothing wakes `W2`.
  - `W1` then closes the cycle. It requests UPDATE mode on a row where `W2` holds NO KEY UPDATE and other transactions keep taking and releasing KEY SHARE (FK checks from child inserts, compatible with `W2`'s mode).
  - `W1`'s `wait_for` is registered on every KEY SHARE holder, so each of their commits wakes `W1`, which retries, conflicts again and starts a new wait.
  - While those commits come less than `deadlock_timeout` apart (1 s by default), `W1` never searches, and the cycle stands.
- **PostgreSQL detects this deadlock.** `MultiXactIdWait` waits on the members one xid at a time, and its wait on `W2`'s xid is not cut short by other commits.
- **Inherited, not new.** v7.8 §6 says "After `deadlock_timeout`, the waiter … runs a DFS". v2's `wait_blocking` starts its clock per wait (`began = Instant::now()`, checked once).
- **Not this card's defect.** The card restates the inherited rule faithfully, the first gate's victim analysis assumed persistent waits, and B-2b's card is not yet cut. So it is a follow-up, not REWORK.

**F-b. Queueing's holder clause is broader than PostgreSQL 17** (NIT; §6.3 "Queueing" says "as PostgreSQL 17 grants").
- **The card's clause.** A new request waits behind each earlier conflicting request "unless its txn already holds a lock on that object".
- **What PostgreSQL 17 does.** `JoinWaitQueue` moves a holder ahead only of the first waiter whose requested mode conflicts with the holder's held modes. It grants at once only if the request conflicts neither with the holders nor with the requests queued ahead of that place.
- **Where they differ.** A txn that holds ACCESS SHARE and requests ROW EXCLUSIVE behind a waiting SHARE request: PostgreSQL queues it, and this text passes it.
- **What it changes.** The `t_queue_rule` T5 case holds under both readings, and liveness is unaffected. Either narrow the clause, or list the divergence in §12. The owner's O7 answer may replace the paragraph anyway.
