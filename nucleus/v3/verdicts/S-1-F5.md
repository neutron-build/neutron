## (1) The chain against the three-way boot-id rule: sound. "An ordinary crash never produces a splice" holds

**The proof I checked.** In class C (power cuts and process crashes, with no post-sync damage), the steps are:

1. **Boot ids are unique and strictly increasing.**
   - A boot appends only after its boot meta is durable (§14 step 8).
   - With no lost meta, every boot's `ckpt_id` and id are above every earlier boot's.
2. **What the walk meets after a hole.** Only these can lie at a position the walk reaches past unsynced bytes:
   - preallocation or truncation zeros (magic or CRC stop);
   - recycled content (`lsn` stop);
   - a torn record (CRC stop);
   - an older boot's record (boot-id stop).
3. **None of them reaches the chain check**, because the chain is checked after every stop check.
4. **So the only records compared are ones their writer appended at that position.** Each names, by construction, the writer's previous record (by start, and by its final `crc`).

**The walk's predecessor equals the writer's predecessor:**
- **Within a boot.** Positions are written once.
- **At a boot boundary.** The first record names the recovery's last accepted record. Its bytes lie below the boot checkpoint's `wal_ckpt_end`, which step 1 synced, and no later boot rewrites them, so every later walk that reaches the boundary accepted them identically. That covers a walk from the same meta or a rollback walk from an older meta, because the walk from the older meta reaches the newer meta's `wal_start_lsn` on a record boundary.
- **At a meta.** `wal_start_lsn` is a record start of the meta's own boot or its append position (§12.6). The pair names that position's predecessor in the writer's stream.

**The cases the request names:**
- **Meta rollbacks.** Above. If segments below the rolled-back meta's start were recycled, the older walk stops at the missing segment. That is a stop or I-WAL-COVER, never a splice.
- **Segment hops and exact segment ends.** The predecessor is the `SegmentEnd` or the exact-end record, on both the writer and the reader side (§12.2, §12.5).
- **Short or missing next segment.** The valid end is the next segment's first LSN, and the boot's first record names the `SegmentEnd` or the exact-end record.
- **Recycled segments.** Old content has old `lsn` values, and a rename is durable before any append (SEG-ACK-BEFORE-DURABLE). The pair is compared, never read through, which is correct because the named record may be recycled.
- **Bulk loads (§12.8).** They write no records. The forced checkpoint is an ordinary checkpoint. Nothing here interacts with them.

The draft-2 gate's statement holds for the merged rule: the argument needs no durable truncation.

**A strength the text understates.**
- `crc` covers `prev_crc`, so the chain is transitive: a chain-consistent walk is, up to CRC collisions, a prefix of one writer's history.
- So "undetected only if the predecessor is byte-identical at the same position" in fact means that the two histories agree byte for byte up to the spliced-in record.
- The text is conservative, not wrong.

**Wording gaps, non-blocking (to S-1-F5-F1):**
- **(a) Step 0 "records" `wal_start_prev_crc` at the flip.** That `crc` may not be sealed yet, because sealing happens at the writer.
  - §2.2 correctly says it is final before step 4.
  - Step 0 should say that it fixes *which* record, and that the value is read after step 1.
- **(b) The append-position pair must be read atomically with the append position**, in one entry to the reservation critical section.
  - `Chunk` reservations race the flip (step 0 item 2).
  - Gate mutant WAL-START-PAIR-SPLIT-READ reads them separately, so the pair can name the racing chunk itself, which gives a false splice from the new meta. No named fixture races a chunk with the read.
- **(c) At a boot checkpoint, before any append, "the last record appended" is the recovery's chain head.** That is the last accepted record, or the opened meta's pair, per §12.2's first-record rule. One clause in §2.2.

## (2) Sealing order: consistent with I-ACK-PREFIX; the serialization is CPU-only

- Only `prev_crc` is a late dependency:
  - `lsn` and `prev_lsn` are known at reservation;
  - `len` is fixed at reservation, so the payload is serialised by then.
- With CRC32C's (affine) linearity, the writer can fold `lsn`, `prev_lsn` and `prev_crc` in at reservation, inside the append critical section, at O(log len). Then the chain head `(start, crc)` advances with the position, and no batch's write or sync waits on another batch's I/O.
- Two or more batches stay independently writable and syncable. Acks still wait only on `durable_end` (§12.4 unchanged).
- The text's "may checksum the rest earlier and fold `prev_crc` in last" permits this, and does not mandate any extra serialization.
- One hazard: gate mutant SEAL-STALE-HEAD folds batch 2 with a chain head read before batch 1 sealed. No named chain test runs with two batches in flight. Carry this to S-1-F5-F1, and the design hint to A-6a and A-6c via PL-4.

## (3) Classification: correct

- **Read-write.** A splice gives XX001 before pass 2, with no replay, eviction write or truncation, at or above `wal_ckpt_end` alike. This is consistent with I-WAL-COVER's write-nothing rule.
- **Stop checks.** Every existing stop check keeps its meaning: the chain is checked last, and a stop is a clean end whatever its `prev_lsn`. I-WAL-STALE, I-WAL-COVER, SEG-EXACT-NO-HOP and COVER-AT-EOF are untouched.
- **The cut** is `min(a, prev_lsn)`:
  - an all-ones or `≥ r` `prev_lsn` reduces to `a` by `min` (u64);
  - when the splice is the walk's first record, the cut is the walk's start;
  - for a `prev_lsn` that matches with a `prev_crc` that differs, the cut is the predecessor itself.

  All are correct. By the transitivity above, salvage serves a ts-prefix of the history that the records below the cut form, which is one writer's real history.

**A gap (S-1-F5-F1).** §14.1 says pass 2 replays only records below the cut. It does not say that pass 1's `CommitMarker` set, and the largest-ts record, are trimmed to the cut.
- Pass 1 has already read up to the splice `r`.
- Gate mutant SPLICE-MARKER-PAST-CUT keeps a marker in `[cut, r)`. Pass 2 then applies that transaction's chunks, which lie below the cut, at the marker's ts.
- The frontier then jumps over an unreplayed lower-ts `Commit` in `[cut, marker)`, so the state is not a ts-prefix.
- `t_wal_splice`'s read-only fixture is not required to straddle the cut with a chunked transaction, so the mutant is UNCAUGHT.
- Likewise SPLICE-CUT-SAME-START-KEEP is UNCAUGHT: on an equal `prev_lsn`, it keeps the predecessor. The reason is that the read-only variant is not crossed with the same-start variant.

These touch only the salvage path (A-7d), never the format, so they do not block A-6a.

## (7) The author's finding 2, S-1-F6: real; S-1-F5's text is safe without it

**The trace (class MP with ordinary power cuts; no MW):**
1. Boot B1 recovers from meta j and appends from V. Its first record is torn, and later batches in segment F (past a hop) are synced.
2. B1's boot meta j+1 is LOST.
3. Boot B2 opens j. Its walk stops at V, so its id equals B1's.
4. B2 truncates. The zeroing or unlinking of F is not synced: step 1 syncs only below V, and `sync_data` on V's segment does not reach F.
5. B2 fills V's segment. Its `SegmentEnd` is made durable, its writes into F are not, and a power cut follows.
6. The walk hops to F and meets B1's record at F: the right `lsn`, the same id, accepted.

**Without the chain,** this replays B1's unacked records, with duplicate ts and TxnIds. **With S-1-F5,** it is XX001, unless B1's and B2's records from V on are byte-identical.

**Why S-1-F5's own claims do not depend on S-1-F6:**
- "No splice in class C" needs unique ids, not durable truncation.
- The Residual states the premise and cites S-1-F6.

So S-1-F5 is safe to merge first.

**Also for S-1-F6 (pre-existing; not an S-1-F5 defect).** S-STORE never says where a recovered boot's `durable_end` starts.
- If it starts at the valid end, then:
  1. after a process crash, recovery accepts the previous process's unsynced page-cache records;
  2. the boot checkpoint's step 1 syncs nothing;
  3. the first ack syncs only its own segment;
  4. a power cut then reverts an earlier segment, and the acked commits are lost as a clean stop.
- That is an I-REDO-COMPLETE break in class C. It is never a splice, so `t_wal_chain_crash`'s "no splice" assertion does not catch it.
- E4 sets `st.wal.durable = 0` after recovery (`ckpt.rs:306`), and it also makes truncation durable (`truncate_wal`, G2). Neither is stated in S-STORE.
