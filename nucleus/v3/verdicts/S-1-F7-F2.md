## The implementer's judgment calls

**1. A chain build holds only the leaf; a replace releases `P` once, before both.** Sound.
- *Why not reserve:* right. Learning the MapLeafs of a maximum-size chain's LPIDs needs one MapLeaf per 510 LPIDs at 8 KiB, and the chain may span more than §5.4's per-thread pins; a free cannot reserve at all (its LPIDs are read from the chain). One arm for both keeps one release point.
- *What a reader sees in the gap.* `P` is released only after the split's separators are installed, so every path through `P` is already consistent. The target leaf stays exclusively latched from before the prune to after the free: the old entry, then the new entry with the new chain, is never visible half-done. A reader coupling into that leaf waits on it while holding `P` shared, which delays writers that need `P` exclusively behind the chain I/O. That is the shape the sweep's chain deletes and the in-place next-pointer reads already have (§5.6, §10.4), and A-5's flip-hold measurement covers its length. Readers and writers of the new sibling proceed.
- *Concurrent writers.* Writers to the same leaf wait on its latch; the reserved room cannot be consumed. A sweep pack of this leaf waits on it holding `P`; nothing it reads is stale when it gets the latch.
- *Crash cuts.* The gate is held shared from the first latch to the last and no restart follows the first mutation, so no flip lands between the split, the build, the replace and the free: every image holds all of the operation or none of it, and I-LINK-ATOMIC holds at every cut. A dirty eviction of the released `P` or sibling mid-operation writes complete content to a fresh slot that no image references yet (§4.4). A crash mid-operation loses the in-memory state only.
- *Gap in pinning it:* nothing tests the build half. `t_replace_free_shape` evicts the old chain's MapLeafs, not those of the LPIDs the new chain takes, so a build that keeps `P` and reads its allocations' MapLeafs in place under it survives (paper mutant CHAIN-BUILD-UNDER-P; F1).

**2. §5.6 multi-LPID allocation in one hold, only if every MapLeaf is resident.** Sound, and it is what the card implies: a second allocation after the first would be after a mutation, with no legal move if its MapLeaf were cold. Complete for the split paths: from the prune's marks the insert knows the split point, hence each separator and which exclusively held ancestor overflows, so the full set (leaf, each splitting ancestor, the root split's two pages) is known before any mutation. **If one is not resident:** none is taken, nothing has mutated, the restart is legal; the operation pins those MapLeafs and re-descends. Gaps (F2): the lowest free LPIDs can change across the restart, so a retry can meet another cold MapLeaf (no progress argument is stated); an LPID from `next_lpid++` past the last MapLeaf has no MapLeaf to be "resident"; and "the free-set lock" is new in this draft with no scope (LPIDs only, or slots too) or rank. Pinning under it touches the page table; if eviction allocates a slot under the same lock while holding the page table, the orders invert.

**3. The leaf-only arm reads a cold MapLeaf after releasing the free-set lock.** Sound and necessary. Reading under it would put I/O, and any nested dirty eviction, under a global lock. The taken LPID is out of the free set and unreferenced until the build maps it, and the gate is held, so no image and no other allocator sees it in between.

**4. §6.7 "Freeing" lists settlers (b) and (c).** Accurate to C-T0 v8 §2.4: (b) deletes an aborted head entry inside its write (its conversions are their own stripe-held operation and free nothing), and (c) deletes a dead one. Two gaps (F1): the clause hangs after "unless the new value is the same chain", so "or that settles ..." first reads as a second exception (parseable, since "unless ... that settles" is not grammatical, but it should move before the "unless"). More important, chain-freeing **deletes** outside the sweep are still not covered: settler (a)'s abort cleanup, `T`'s own delete on `ROLLBACK TO` (C-T0 v8 §2.4 "may replace or delete"), and a replayed `Delete{revert}` of a tag-8 entry. Under §6.2's single "safe" rule, a delete into a full leaf holds `P`, and the new sentence names only "an insert that replaces or settles". It is the same §5.3/§5.6 conflict as S-1-F7-F1-F1-F1, pre-existing and outside the card's text.

**8. N4: no backoff on the `try_latch` re-descent.** Safety holds. The re-descent comes before any mutation, keeps no root name (the entry and its state are re-read), and DropTree's last step keeps its pins across it. Liveness has two gaps (F2):
- The text does not say the re-descent is a §5.6 restart, so a writer spinning on a root a cursor holds keeps the gate shared and the flip waits. That is no worse than the old blocking wait, but the restart is a free fix.
- `try_latch` has no queue. Every writer takes its tree's root exclusively and every operation takes it, so a writer can starve on a root that a steady stream of readers keeps shared. That hazard is new with N4.

Also, `DIR-LEAF-EXCLUSIVE` is not reached as the test text says ("Y's writer serialises behind X's"). Under `try_latch`, X's writer holds the directory leaf only for one failed attempt, so with a fair latch Y finishes a few steps later. Only an artificially tight budget catches it. The hook needs a mode assertion: an operation that does not change the directory holds its directory leaf shared. `ROOT-WAIT-HOLDING-DIR` is fine, because the hook catches it deterministically.
